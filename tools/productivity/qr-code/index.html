What is a QR Code and How Does It Function?

Invented in 1994 by the Japanese automotive company Denso Wave to track auto parts, the QR code (Quick Response code) is a two-dimensional matrix barcode. Unlike standard one-dimensional UPC barcodes that encode data across a single horizontal line of varying widths, QR codes store information in both horizontal and vertical directions across square grid modules.

Reed-Solomon Error Correction Explained

A defining feature of QR codes is built-in Reed-Solomon error correction. This mathematical algorithm allows physical QR codes to be cleanly decoded even if up to 30% of the symbol is obscured, smudged, torn, or covered by a decorative brand logo.

  • Level L (Low): Approximately 7% of damaged codewords can be restored.
  • Level M (Medium): Approximately 15% error correction (standard for general web links).
  • Level Q (Quartile): Approximately 25% error correction.
  • Level H (High): Up to 30% error correction (ideal for high-wear outdoor posters and restaurant menus).
