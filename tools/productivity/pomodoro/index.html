What is the Pomodoro Technique?

Developed in the late 1980s by Francesco Cirillo, the Pomodoro Technique is a time-management methodology that uses a timer to break work into focused intervals—traditionally 25 minutes in length—separated by short 5-minute breaks. Each 25-minute work interval is known as a pomodoro (the Italian word for tomato, named after the tomato-shaped kitchen timer Cirillo originally used).

The Standard 4-Step Pomodoro Cycle

  1. Select a Single Task: Eliminate all multi-tasking and dedicate your full attention to one clear objective.
  2. Work for 25 Minutes: Focus relentlessly until the timer chime sounds.
  3. Take a 5-Minute Break: Step away from the screen, stretch, hydrate, or rest your eyes.
  4. Long Break Every 4 Sessions: After completing four consecutive focus sessions, take an extended 15-to-30-minute break to recharge.

Why Browser-Based Focus Timers Work

By creating artificial urgency through countdown intervals, the Pomodoro technique counters Parkinson's Law (the adage that work expands to fill the time available for its completion).
