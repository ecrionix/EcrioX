Law of Conservation of Mass

\sum \text{Atoms}_{\text{Reactants}} = \sum \text{Atoms}_{\text{Products}}, \quad \mathbf{A} \cdot \vec{x} = \vec{0}

Matter can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. Stoichiometric balancing constructs a homogeneous matrix system where coefficients x represent the integer nullspace.

Chemical equations symbolize the atomic rearrangements that occur during chemical transformations. Antoine Lavoisier formulated the Law of Conservation of Mass in 1789, proving that mass remains constant in closed chemical systems. Subscripts within a chemical formula represent chemical bonding and must never be altered when balancing. Instead, integer stoichiometric coefficients are placed before formulas to balance the equation.

Worked Example & Step-by-Step Calculation

Problem Statement:

Balance the redox combustion equation: C₄H₁₀ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O.

Mathematical Solution & Derivation:

1. Count carbon atoms: 4 carbons on left → place coefficient 4 before CO₂: C₄H₁₀ + O₂ → 4 CO₂ + H₂O. 2. Count hydrogen atoms: 10 hydrogens on left → place coefficient 5 before H₂O: C₄H₁₀ + O₂ → 4 CO₂ + 5 H₂O. 3. Count oxygen atoms on right: (4 × 2) + (5 × 1) = 13 oxygen atoms. 4. Required O₂ on left is 13/2 = 6.5: C₄H₁₀ + 13/2 O₂ → 4 CO₂ + 5 H₂O. 5. Multiply all coefficients by 2 to obtain lowest integers: 2 C₄H₁₀ + 13 O₂ → 8 CO₂ + 10 H₂O.

Common Student Misconceptions & Scientific Reality

💡 Critical Conceptual Insight: A major error is changing chemical subscripts to balance equations (e.g., writing H₂ + O₂ → H₂O₂ instead of 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O). Changing subscripts alters the chemical identity of the substance.
