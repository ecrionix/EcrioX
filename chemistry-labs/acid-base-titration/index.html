Equivalence Point & Neutralization Stoichiometry

M_A V_A = M_B V_B, \quad \text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + \log_{10}\left(\frac{[\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]}\right)

At the stoichiometric equivalence point, moles of H⁺ equal moles of OH⁻. For weak acid titrations, the buffer region is modeled by the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation.

Titration is a fundamental quantitative analytical chemistry technique used to determine the unknown concentration of an identified analyte. A standardized solution (titrant) is added from a burette to a known volume of analyte until the reaction reaches completion. The equivalence point occurs when chemically equivalent amounts of acid and base have reacted. The endpoint is the observed visual signal (such as an indicator color change) indicating that the equivalence point has been reached.

Experimental Objectives

  • Master standard quantitative volumetric analysis techniques.
  • Select appropriate acid-base indicators (Phenolphthalein, Methyl Orange, Bromothymol Blue) matching equivalence pH.
  • Plot and interpret sigmoidal titration curves and calculate buffer capacity.
  • Calculate unknown analyte molarity from standardized titrant volume.

Step-by-Step Laboratory Procedure

  1. Step 1:

    Select your analyte acid (e.g. 0.1 M HCl or 0.1 M CH3COOH) and volume (25.0 mL).

  2. Step 2:

    Add an indicator: Phenolphthalein (colorless to pink) or Methyl Orange (red to yellow).

  3. Step 3:

    Open the burette stopcock to add standardized 0.1 M NaOH dropwise or in rapid increments.

  4. Step 4:

    Observe the color transition and locate the inflection point on the live pH vs volume curve.

  5. Step 5:

    Note the equivalence volume V_eq and compute the unknown analyte concentration.

Worked Example & Step-by-Step Calculation

Problem Statement:

A 25.0 mL sample of unknown hydrochloric acid (HCl) is titrated with standardized 0.120 M sodium hydroxide (NaOH). The phenolphthalein endpoint is reached after adding 31.25 mL of NaOH. Calculate the molarity of the HCl sample.

Mathematical Solution & Derivation:

1. Balanced neutralization equation: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O (1:1 mole ratio). 2. Calculate moles of NaOH consumed: n_base = M_base × V_base = 0.120 mol/L × 0.03125 L = 0.00375 mol. 3. At equivalence: n_acid = n_base = 0.00375 mol. 4. Calculate molarity of HCl: M_acid = n_acid / V_acid = 0.00375 mol / 0.0250 L = 0.150 M.

Common Student Misconceptions & Scientific Reality

💡 Critical Conceptual Insight: Students often assume the equivalence point of any acid-base titration is always at pH 7.0. While strong acid-strong base titrations reach equivalence at pH 7.0, weak acid-strong base titrations produce basic salt hydrolysis, reaching equivalence at pH > 7 (e.g. pH 8.7 for acetic acid).

Frequently Asked Questions on Acid-Base Titration Virtual Laboratory

The equivalence point is the theoretical state where stoichiometric moles of titrant equal moles of analyte. The endpoint is the experimentally observed signal (such as an indicator color change) indicating titration completion.

Phenolphthalein changes color between pH 8.2 and 10.0, which perfectly overlaps the basic equivalence point inflection zone resulting from conjugate base hydrolysis.

At half-equivalence, half of the weak acid has been converted to its conjugate base ([HA] = [A⁻]). By the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation, pH = pKa + log(1) = pKa.
