Types of Chemical Isomerism

\text{Constitutional Isomers} \ne \text{Stereoisomers}, \quad \Delta T_b \propto \text{Surface Area}

Isomers share identical molecular formulas. Constitutional isomers differ in atomic connectivity. Stereoisomers share connectivity but differ in spatial orientation.

Isomers are compounds that have the exact same molecular formula but different chemical structures and physical properties. Constitutional (structural) isomers differ in the connectivity of their atoms: chain isomers differ in carbon backbone branching (butane vs isobutane); position isomers differ in the location of a functional group (1-propanol vs 2-propanol); functional isomers contain entirely different functional groups (ethanol, an alcohol, vs dimethyl ether, an ether). Stereoisomers have the same atomic connectivity but differ in 3D spatial orientation. Geometric (cis-trans) isomerism occurs in alkenes because the carbon-carbon double bond has restricted rotation.

Experimental Objectives

  • Distinguish constitutional isomers from stereoisomers.
  • Analyze structural isomers: chain isomerism, position isomerism, and functional group isomerism.
  • Explore geometric cis-trans isomerism arising from restricted rotation about carbon-carbon double bonds.
  • Evaluate how structural branching alters physical properties such as boiling point and melting point.

Step-by-Step Laboratory Procedure

  1. Step 1:

    Choose an isomerism category: Chain, Position, Functional Group, or Geometric (Cis/Trans).

  2. Step 2:

  3. Step 3:

  4. Step 4:

    Compare the physical property table (boiling points, melting points, dipole moments) explaining structure-property relationships.

Worked Example & Step-by-Step Calculation

Problem Statement:

Explain why n-Pentane has a higher boiling point (36.1°C) than its structural isomer Neopentane / 2,2-dimethylpropane (9.5°C), despite having the identical molecular formula C₅H₁₂.

Mathematical Solution & Derivation:

1. Both compounds share the identical formula C₅H₁₂ and molecular mass (72.15 g/mol). 2. n-Pentane has an extended, linear zig-zag carbon chain with a large molecular surface area. - Greater surface area permits extensive London dispersion intermolecular interactions. - Boiling point is relatively high: 36.1°C. 3. Neopentane is highly branched and adopts a compact, nearly spherical shape. - Spherical geometry minimizes surface area of contact between adjacent molecules. - London dispersion forces are significantly weaker. - Substantially lower thermal energy is required to vaporize Neopentane: boiling point is only 9.5°C.

Polymer Engineering

High-density polyethylene (linear chains) vs low-density polyethylene (branched chains).

Food Industry

Cis-unsaturated fats vs trans-fats and their effects on cardiovascular health.

Common Student Misconceptions & Scientific Reality

💡 Critical Conceptual Insight: Students often believe that rotating a single bond creates an isomer. Conformations produced by rotation around single sigma bonds are conformational isomers (conformers), which interconvert freely at room temperature and are not separate chemical compounds.
