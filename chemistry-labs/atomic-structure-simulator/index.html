Atomic Mass & Subatomic Relationships

A = Z + N, \quad Q = Z - e, \quad \text{Capacity} = 2n^2

Atomic number Z equals the proton count and defines the chemical element. Mass number A is protons plus neutrons. Electron shell n holds a maximum of 2n² electrons (K=2, L=8, M=18, N=32).

Atoms consist of a dense, positively charged nucleus surrounded by a cloud of negative electrons. Protons carry a +1 elementary charge (1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C) and mass ~1.0073 u. Neutrons are uncharged with mass ~1.0087 u. Electrons possess a -1 charge with 1/1836 the mass of a proton. While the Bohr model simplifies electron motion into circular planar orbits with capacities 2, 8, 18, it provides a strong foundation for understanding valence electrons and Lewis octets before advancing to quantum mechanical wavefunctions (s, p, d, f orbitals).

Worked Example & Step-by-Step Calculation

Problem Statement:

Determine the number of protons, neutrons, and electrons in an aluminum ion: ²⁷₁₃Al³⁺.

Mathematical Solution & Derivation:

1. Atomic number Z = 13, therefore the nucleus contains 13 protons. 2. Mass number A = 27. Number of neutrons N = A - Z = 27 - 13 = 14 neutrons. 3. Net charge Q = +3. Number of electrons e = Z - Q = 13 - 3 = 10 electrons. 4. Electron configuration of Al³⁺: 2, 8 (isoelectronic with Neon).

Common Student Misconceptions & Scientific Reality

💡 Critical Conceptual Insight: Students frequently assume that changing the number of electrons changes the element. Only changing the number of nuclear protons changes the element's identity. Gaining or losing electrons creates ions of the same element.
