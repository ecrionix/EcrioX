Integrated Rate Laws & Arrhenius Equation

\ln[A]_t = -kt + \ln[A]_0, \quad t_{1/2} = \frac{\ln 2}{k}, \quad k = A e^{-\frac{E_a}{RT}}

First-order reactions exhibit exponential decay with constant half-life independent of initial concentration. The Arrhenius equation models exponential rate constant sensitivity to absolute temperature.

Chemical kinetics investigates the rates of chemical reactions and the molecular pathways (mechanisms) by which they occur. Collision Theory states that for a reaction to occur, reactant particles must collide with energy greater than or equal to the activation energy (E_a) and with appropriate molecular orientation. The reaction order describes the mathematical dependency of rate on reactant concentration. For a first-order process, rate = k[A], and the half-life t₁/₂ = ln(2)/k is uniquely independent of initial concentration.

Worked Example & Step-by-Step Calculation

Problem Statement:

The first-order decomposition of dinitrogen pentoxide (N₂O₅) has a rate constant k = 6.2 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹ at 45°C. Calculate: (a) The reaction half-life, (b) The time required for the concentration to fall to 12.5% of its initial value.

Mathematical Solution & Derivation:

1. Half-life formula for first-order reaction: t₁/₂ = ln(2) / k = 0.69315 / (6.2 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹) = 1118 seconds (≈ 18.6 minutes). 2. Calculate elapsed half-lives to reach 12.5%: 100% → 50% (1 half-life) → 25% (2 half-lives) → 12.5% (3 half-lives). n = 3 half-lives. 3. Total time required: t = 3 × t₁/₂ = 3 × 1118 s = 3354 seconds (≈ 55.9 minutes).

Common Student Misconceptions & Scientific Reality

💡 Critical Conceptual Insight: Students often assume that reaction order can be determined directly from the stoichiometric coefficients of an overall balanced equation. Reaction order is an experimentally determined quantity that depends on the slowest elementary step (rate-determining step), not overall stoichiometry.

Frequently Asked Questions on Chemical Kinetics & Reaction Rates Simulator

In a first-order integrated rate law, t₁/₂ = ln(2) / k. The initial concentration term [A]₀ cancels out entirely, meaning it takes the exact same duration to decay from 1.0 M to 0.5 M as it does from 0.001 M to 0.0005 M.

A catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy (E_a). By the Arrhenius equation, lowering E_a exponentially increases the rate constant k without consuming the catalyst.

Zero order: mol/(L·s); First order: s⁻¹; Second order: L/(mol·s). In general: (mol/L)^(1-n) · s⁻¹ where n is reaction order.
