Standard Cell Potential & Nernst Equation

E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cathode}} - E^\circ_{\text{anode}}, \quad E_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cell}} - \frac{0.0592}{n}\log_{10} Q

Electrons flow spontaneously from anode (oxidation, -) to cathode (reduction, +). The Nernst equation models how non-standard ion concentrations alter cell voltage.

A Galvanic (Voltaic) cell converts chemical energy from a spontaneous redox reaction into electrical energy. Oxidation (loss of electrons) occurs at the anode (labeled negative in galvanic cells). Reduction (gain of electrons) occurs at the cathode (labeled positive). Electrons travel through the external wire circuit from anode to cathode. To maintain electrical neutrality, a salt bridge containing an inert electrolyte (such as KNO₃ or KCl) connects the solutions: anions migrate into the anode beaker to balance accumulating metal cations, while cations migrate into the cathode beaker.

Experimental Objectives

  • Identify the anode (oxidation) and cathode (reduction) using the electrochemical series.
  • Demonstrate the critical electrical neutrality function of the U-tube salt bridge.
  • Calculate standard cell potential (E°cell) from standard reduction potentials.
  • Apply the Nernst equation to predict voltage variations under non-standard concentrations.

Step-by-Step Laboratory Procedure

  1. Step 1:

    Select anode and cathode metal half-cells (e.g. Zinc anode in ZnSO₄ and Copper cathode in CuSO₄ for a Daniell Cell).

  2. Step 2:

    Adjust the electrolyte molar concentrations in both beakers (0.01 M to 2.0 M).

  3. Step 3:

    Observe the animated voltmeter reading, light bulb glow, and electron flow direction.

  4. Step 4:

    Watch the salt bridge anions (NO₃⁻) migrate to the anode and cations (K⁺) migrate to the cathode.

Worked Example & Step-by-Step Calculation

Problem Statement:

Consider a Daniell cell: Zn(s) | Zn²⁺(aq, 0.10 M) || Cu²⁺(aq, 1.0 M) | Cu(s). Given standard reduction potentials E°(Zn²⁺/Zn) = -0.76 V and E°(Cu²⁺/Cu) = +0.34 V at 25°C, calculate: (a) Standard cell potential E°cell, (b) Cell potential E_cell under the given concentrations.

Mathematical Solution & Derivation:

1. Identify half-reactions: - Anode (oxidation): Zn(s) → Zn²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ [E° = -0.76 V]. - Cathode (reduction): Cu²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ → Cu(s) [E° = +0.34 V]. 2. Calculate standard cell potential: E°cell = E°cathode - E°anode = +0.34 V - (-0.76 V) = +1.10 V. 3. Identify number of transferred electrons: n = 2. 4. Write reaction quotient Q: Q = [Zn²⁺] / [Cu²⁺] = 0.10 / 1.0 = 0.10. 5. Apply Nernst equation at 25°C: E_cell = E°cell - (0.0592 / n) × log₁₀(Q) E_cell = 1.10 V - (0.0592 / 2) × log₁₀(0.10) E_cell = 1.10 V - (0.0296 × (-1.0)) = 1.10 V + 0.0296 V = 1.13 V.
