Faraday's Laws of Electrolysis

m = \frac{M \cdot I \cdot t}{n \cdot F}, \quad Q = I \cdot t, \quad F = 96485 \text{ C/mol e}^-

The mass m of an element deposited or liberated at an electrode is directly proportional to electric charge Q (current I × time t) and molar mass M, and inversely proportional to electron valency n.

Electrolysis is the process by which electrical energy from an external DC power source forces a non-spontaneous redox reaction to occur (ΔG > 0). The power supply acts as an electron pump, pulling electrons away from the anode (making it positive in electrolytic cells) and forcing electrons onto the cathode (making it negative). Michael Faraday in 1834 established that the amount of chemical change produced is strictly proportional to the total electrical charge passed. One mole of electrons carries approximately 96,485 Coulombs of charge (Faraday's constant F = e × N_A).

Worked Example & Step-by-Step Calculation

Problem Statement:

A constant current of 3.00 A is passed through an aqueous copper(II) sulfate (CuSO₄) solution for 45.0 minutes. Calculate the mass of copper metal deposited on the cathode. (Molar mass of Cu = 63.55 g/mol, F = 96,485 C/mol).

Mathematical Solution & Derivation:

1. Cathode half-reaction: Cu²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ → Cu(s). Number of electrons n = 2. 2. Convert time to seconds: t = 45.0 min × 60 s/min = 2700 s. 3. Calculate total charge passed: Q = I × t = 3.00 A × 2700 s = 8100 Coulombs. 4. Calculate moles of electrons: n_e = Q / F = 8100 C / 96485 C/mol = 0.08395 mol e⁻. 5. Apply Faraday's Law: m = (M × Q) / (n × F) = (63.55 g/mol × 8100 C) / (2 × 96485 C/mol) m = 514,755 / 192,970 = 2.668 grams of copper deposited.

Common Student Misconceptions & Scientific Reality

💡 Critical Conceptual Insight: Students frequently confuse electrode polarity between galvanic and electrolytic cells. In an electrolytic cell, the ANODE is connected to the positive terminal of the power supply (+), and the CATHODE is connected to the negative terminal (-). However, OXIDATION ALWAYS occurs at the anode in both cell types.

Frequently Asked Questions on Electrolysis & Faraday's Laws Simulator

The overall balanced equation is 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂. For every 4 moles of electrons transferred, 2 moles of H₂ gas are generated at the cathode while only 1 mole of O₂ gas is generated at the anode, creating an exact 2:1 volume ratio.

Overpotential is the extra voltage beyond theoretical thermodynamic cell potential required to overcome kinetic activation barriers (sluggish electron transfer) at electrode surfaces, particularly for gas-evolving reactions like O₂.

In aqueous NaCl, water is reduced much more easily than Na⁺ (reduction potential of H₂O is -0.83 V vs Na⁺ at -2.71 V). Electrolysis of aqueous NaCl produces hydrogen gas at the cathode, not sodium metal.
