VSEPR Theory & Bond Angle Predictions

\text{Steric Number} = \text{Lone Pairs} + \text{Bonded Atoms}, \quad \theta_{\text{tetrahedral}} = 109.47^\circ

Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion (VSEPR) predicts molecular geometry by minimizing electrostatic repulsion between valence electron pairs around a central atom.

Molecules are three-dimensional physical objects whose chemical and biological functions are dictated by spatial conformation. The Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion (VSEPR) model predicts geometry based on the principle that valence electron pairs surrounding a central atom repel each other. Repulsion strength follows the order: Lone Pair-Lone Pair > Lone Pair-Bonding Pair > Bonding Pair-Bonding Pair. For example, in water (H₂O), the oxygen atom has steric number 4 (two bonding pairs and two lone pairs). The two bulky lone pairs compress the ideal tetrahedral angle (109.5°) down to 104.5°.

Worked Example & Step-by-Step Calculation

Problem Statement:

Predict the electron-pair geometry, molecular geometry, and bond angle of Ammonia (NH₃) using VSEPR theory.

Mathematical Solution & Derivation:

1. Draw Lewis structure: Central Nitrogen has 5 valence electrons + 3 from Hydrogens = 8 electrons. 2. Count electron domains around Nitrogen: - 3 single N-H covalent bonds. - 1 non-bonding lone pair. 3. Steric Number = 3 + 1 = 4. 4. Electron-pair geometry: Tetrahedral (steric number 4). 5. Molecular geometry: Trigonal Pyramidal (accounting for atoms only). 6. Bond angle: The lone pair exerts greater repulsive force than bonding pairs, compressing the ideal 109.5° tetrahedral angle down to 107.3°.

Common Student Misconceptions & Scientific Reality

💡 Critical Conceptual Insight: A widespread error is confusing electron-pair geometry with molecular geometry. Electron-pair geometry describes the spatial arrangement of ALL electron domains (including lone pairs). Molecular geometry describes ONLY the positions of bonded atoms.
