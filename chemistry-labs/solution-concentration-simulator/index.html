Solution Concentration & Dilution Formulas

M = \frac{n_{\text{solute}}}{V_{\text{solution}} (\text{L})}, \quad m = \frac{n_{\text{solute}}}{m_{\text{solvent}} (\text{kg})}, \quad C_1 V_1 = C_2 V_2

Molarity (M) expresses solute moles per liter of solution. Molality (m) expresses solute moles per kilogram of solvent. Adding solvent preserves total solute moles during dilution.

A solution is a homogeneous mixture of two or more substances. The substance present in the smaller amount is the solute, while the dispersing medium is the solvent. Molarity (M) is temperature-dependent because liquid volume expands with temperature. Molality (m) depends only on masses of solute and solvent, making it independent of temperature and ideal for colligative property measurements (freezing point depression, boiling point elevation).

Worked Example & Step-by-Step Calculation

Problem Statement:

How many milliliters of 6.00 M stock hydrochloric acid (HCl) are required to prepare 500 mL of 0.250 M dilute HCl solution?

Mathematical Solution & Derivation:

1. Use the dilution formula: C₁V₁ = C₂V₂. 2. Identify known parameters: - Stock concentration C₁ = 6.00 M. - Dilute concentration C₂ = 0.250 M. - Dilute volume V₂ = 500 mL. 3. Rearrange for required stock volume V₁: V₁ = (C₂ × V₂) / C₁ = (0.250 M × 500 mL) / 6.00 M = 125 / 6.00 = 20.83 mL. 4. Procedure: Carefully measure 20.83 mL of 6.00 M HCl and add to ~300 mL of water, then dilute to 500 mL.

Common Student Misconceptions & Scientific Reality

💡 Critical Conceptual Insight: Students frequently confuse solution volume with solvent volume when calculating molarity. 1 liter of solution is the total volume of solute PLUS solvent combined, not 1 liter of pure water added to the solute.

Frequently Asked Questions on Solution Concentration & Dilution Simulator

Molality relies on solvent mass (kg), which is completely unaffected by temperature fluctuations. Molarity relies on volume, which expands and contracts with temperature changes.

The solution becomes saturated. Any additional solute added will not dissolve and settles at the bottom of the container as solid precipitate.

Always add acid to water ('AAA' rule), never water to acid. Concentrated acid hydration is intensely exothermic; adding water to acid causes localized boiling and dangerous corrosive acid spattering.
