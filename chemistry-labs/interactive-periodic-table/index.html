Periodic Law & Effective Nuclear Charge

Z_{\text{eff}} = Z - S, \quad \chi = \frac{I + E_{\text{ea}}}{2}

Moseley's Periodic Law states that elemental properties are periodic functions of atomic number Z. Slater's rules calculate effective nuclear charge Z_eff by subtracting shielding constant S.

In 1869, Dmitri Mendeleev arranged 63 known elements by atomic mass and predicted the properties of undiscovered elements. Henry Moseley in 1913 proved that atomic number (the number of nuclear protons) governs elemental periodicity. The modern periodic table arranges 118 elements across 7 periods and 18 groups. Elements in the same group possess identical valence electron configurations, creating analogous chemical reactivity (such as the univalent alkali metals or inert noble gas octets). Periodic trends arise from the balance between nuclear charge attraction and electron-electron shielding.

Worked Example & Step-by-Step Calculation

Problem Statement:

Calculate the effective nuclear charge (Z_eff) experienced by a 4s valence electron in neutral Potassium (K, Z = 19) using Slater's Rules.

Mathematical Solution & Derivation:

1. Electron configuration of Potassium (Z = 19): (1s)² (2s, 2p)⁸ (3s, 3p)⁸ (4s)¹. 2. Identify shielding groups for a 4s electron: - Electrons in the same (4s) group: 0 (the test electron is excluded). - Electrons in the (n-1) shell (3s, 3p): 8 electrons, each contributing 0.85. Shielding S1 = 8 × 0.85 = 6.80. - Electrons in (n-2) and deeper shells (1s, 2s, 2p): 10 electrons, each contributing 1.00. Shielding S2 = 10 × 1.00 = 10.00. 3. Total shielding constant S = 6.80 + 10.00 = 16.80. 4. Effective nuclear charge: Z_eff = Z - S = 19 - 16.80 = +2.20.

Common Student Misconceptions & Scientific Reality

💡 Critical Conceptual Insight: A frequent misconception is that atomic radius always increases with atomic number. In reality, moving left-to-right across a period increases nuclear charge without adding new shells, pulling valence electrons tighter and causing atomic radius to decrease.
