Water Autoionization & pH Definition

\text{pH} = -\log_{10}[\text{H}^+], \quad K_w = [\text{H}^+][\text{OH}^-] = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}, \quad \text{pH} + \text{pOH} = 14

In pure water at 25°C, autoionization yields equal concentrations [H+] = [OH-] = 10⁻⁷ M (pH = 7). Each unit change in pH represents a ten-fold (10×) change in hydrogen ion concentration.

Søren Sørensen introduced the pH scale in 1909 to quantify acidity. Because hydrogen ion concentrations in aqueous solutions span many orders of magnitude (from over 1 M down to 10⁻¹⁴ M), a logarithmic scale is employed. Because pH = -log₁₀[H⁺], a solution with pH 3 has 10 times more [H⁺] than a solution with pH 4, and 100 times more than pH 5. In any aqueous solution at 25°C, the product of [H⁺] and [OH⁻] is constrained by the water autoionization constant Kw = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴.

Experimental Objectives

  • Demonstrate the inverse logarithmic relationship between pH and hydronium concentration [H⁺].
  • Verify the ion product of water Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 25°C.
  • Classify substances as strongly acidic (0-3), weakly acidic (3-7), neutral (7), weakly basic (7-11), or strongly basic (11-14).
  • Interpret color shifts using universal indicator and digital pH electrodes.

Worked Example & Step-by-Step Calculation

Problem Statement:

Calculate the pH, pOH, and [OH⁻] of a 0.0025 M hydrochloric acid (HCl) solution at 25°C.

Mathematical Solution & Derivation:

1. Hydrochloric acid is a strong monoprotic acid that dissociates completely: [H⁺] = 0.0025 M = 2.5 × 10⁻³ M. 2. Calculate pH: pH = -log₁₀(2.5 × 10⁻³) = -(-2.602) = 2.60. 3. Calculate pOH: pOH = 14 - pH = 14 - 2.60 = 11.40. 4. Calculate [OH⁻]: [OH⁻] = 10^(-11.40) = 3.98 × 10⁻¹² M.

Common Student Misconceptions & Scientific Reality

💡 Critical Conceptual Insight: A common misconception is that pH cannot be negative or greater than 14. Concentrated acids (such as 12 M HCl) have [H⁺] > 1 M, yielding negative pH values (pH ≈ -1.08). The 0-14 boundary applies primarily to dilute aqueous systems.
