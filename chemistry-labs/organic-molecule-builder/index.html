Degrees of Unsaturation (IHD) & Valence Rules

\text{IHD} = C - \frac{H}{2} + \frac{N}{2} + 1, \quad \text{Valency: } C=4, N=3, O=2, H=1

Index of Hydrogen Deficiency (IHD) calculates the total number of rings plus pi bonds from the molecular formula. Common organic heteroatoms follow standard Lewis octet valencies.

Organic chemistry is centered on the unique ability of carbon to form four strong covalent bonds (catenation), creating stable chains, rings, and complex three-dimensional architectures. Kekulé structural formulas illustrate the connectivity of atoms (constitution) using lines for electron-pair bonds. Because atoms seek stable valence octets, carbon reliably forms four bonds (sp³, sp², or sp hybridized), nitrogen forms three bonds with one lone pair, oxygen forms two bonds with two lone pairs, and hydrogen forms exactly one single bond.

Worked Example & Step-by-Step Calculation

Problem Statement:

Calculate the Index of Hydrogen Deficiency (IHD / degrees of unsaturation) for benzoic acid: C₇H₆O₂.

Mathematical Solution & Derivation:

1. Standard IHD formula: IHD = C - (H/2) + (N/2) + 1. 2. For C₇H₆O₂: C = 7, H = 6, N = 0, O is ignored in IHD formula. 3. Calculate: IHD = 7 - (6/2) + 0 + 1 = 7 - 3 + 1 = 5. 4. Structural interpretation: Benzoic acid contains 5 degrees of unsaturation: - 1 benzene ring (1 degree) - 3 aromatic carbon-carbon double bonds (3 degrees) - 1 carbonyl (C=O) double bond in the carboxylic acid group (1 degree). Total = 1 + 3 + 1 = 5.

Common Student Misconceptions & Scientific Reality

💡 Critical Conceptual Insight: A frequent error among beginner chemistry students is drawing 'pentavalent carbon' (a carbon atom with five bonds, nicknamed the 'Texas Carbon'). Carbon only has four valence orbitals (2s and three 2p) and can never exceed eight valence electrons.

Frequently Asked Questions on 2D Organic Molecule Builder & Valence Validator

A molecular formula (e.g. C₂H₆O) states the total atom counts without showing connectivity. A structural formula illustrates how atoms are bonded, distinguishing isomers like ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH) from dimethyl ether (CH₃OCH₃).

Carbon has 4 valence electrons and requires 4 more to complete its octet (forming 4 covalent bonds). Nitrogen has 5 valence electrons and requires 3 more (forming 3 covalent bonds and retaining 1 lone pair).

One degree of unsaturation (IHD = 1) signifies the absence of two hydrogen atoms relative to an open-chain saturated alkane, corresponding to either one ring or one double bond.
