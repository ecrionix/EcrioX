Reaction Enthalpy & Activation Energy

\Delta H = \sum H_{\text{products}} - \sum H_{\text{reactants}}, \quad E_a = E_{\text{transition}} - E_{\text{reactants}}

Exothermic reactions release heat (ΔH < 0), whereas endothermic reactions absorb heat (ΔH > 0). Activation energy E_a represents the kinetic threshold required to reach the transition state.

Chemical reactions occur when reactant particles collide with sufficient kinetic energy (greater than or equal to activation energy E_a) and correct steric orientation to break chemical bonds and form new products. Synthesis combines simpler substances into a compound (A + B → AB). Decomposition breaks a compound into simpler constituents (AB → A + B). Single displacement involves an active element replacing a less active element (A + BC → AC + B), governed by the electrochemical activity series. Double displacement involves mutual exchange of ions (AB + CD → AD + CB).

Worked Example & Step-by-Step Calculation

Problem Statement:

When aqueous barium chloride (BaCl₂) is mixed with aqueous sodium sulfate (Na₂SO₄), a white precipitate forms. Write the molecular, complete ionic, and net ionic equations.

Mathematical Solution & Derivation:

1. Molecular equation: BaCl₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq) → BaSO₄(s)↓ + 2 NaCl(aq). 2. Complete ionic equation: Ba²⁺(aq) + 2 Cl⁻(aq) + 2 Na⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s) + 2 Na⁺(aq) + 2 Cl⁻(aq). 3. Cancel spectator ions (Na⁺ and Cl⁻): Net ionic equation: Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s)↓.

Common Student Misconceptions & Scientific Reality

💡 Critical Conceptual Insight: Students often assume all double displacement reactions form precipitates. Double displacement reactions also occur when an un-ionized weak electrolyte (such as water in acid-base neutralization) or an insoluble gas (such as CO₂ or H₂S) is produced.

Frequently Asked Questions on Chemical Reaction Types & Energy Simulator

Spectator ions are ions that exist in the same state on both the reactant and product sides of a chemical equation without participating in the actual chemical change.

By referencing the electrochemical activity series: an element can only displace another element from a compound if it is located higher in the activity series.

Exothermic reactions release thermal energy to the surroundings (ΔH is negative, temperature rises), while endothermic reactions absorb heat (ΔH is positive, temperature drops).
