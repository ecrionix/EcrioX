1. Fixed Coordinates vs Flowing Text: The Architectural Clash

To understand why PDF conversion is difficult, consider how both formats think about text:

  • PDF (Fixed Presentation Model): A PDF document has no concept of a "sentence" or a "paragraph." It contains drawing instructions that place text fragments at exact spatial coordinates: e.g., Draw the word "Agreement" at X: 72.0, Y: 750.4 with font Helvetica 14pt. If you add three words, there is no automatic reflow; words simply collide.
  • WordprocessingML / DOCX (Flowing Content Model): Word documents are structured XML trees composed of paragraphs (<w:p>), runs (<w:r>), and text nodes (<w:t>). Text flows naturally between page margins, wrapping around figures and pushing content onto subsequent pages.

A quality converter analyzes character bounding boxes, vertical baselines, and horizontal kerning spaces to reverse-engineer continuous sentences from scattered coordinate fragments.

2. Native Digital PDFs vs Scanned Bitmap PDFs (OCR)

Before converting, it is essential to determine whether your PDF is digital or scanned:

  • Native Digital PDFs: Created by "Save As PDF" from Microsoft Word, Google Docs, Adobe InDesign, or browser print dialogs. These contain selectable, copyable vector character codes and convert with near-flawless accuracy.
  • Scanned Raster PDFs: Created when physical paper is run through an office copy machine or photographed with a phone. The PDF contains only high-resolution bitmap images of text, with zero underlying character streams. These require Optical Character Recognition (OCR) to reconstruct glyphs before they can become editable words.
Quick Test: Click and drag your mouse across a line of text in the PDF. If the text highlights in blue, it is a native digital PDF. If your cursor drags a box or nothing highlights, it is a raster scan.

4. Preserving Tables, Headers & Multi-Column Layouts

Tables and multi-column magazine layouts represent the biggest challenge in PDF conversion. In a PDF, table borders are drawn as independent vector path strokes (<code>moveTo</code> and <code>lineTo</code>), while cells are unlinked text blocks floating nearby.

When opening your converted document:

  • Check Tabular Alignments: If a complex multi-level financial ledger was converted, inspect cell margins. Adjust column widths in Microsoft Word by dragging table dividers.
  • Header and Footer Flow: Repetitive running headers and page numbers are grouped cleanly into separate top and bottom paragraph blocks.
