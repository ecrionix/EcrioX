1. When and Why Should You Merge PDF Files?

In corporate, legal, and academic environments, sending five separate email attachments creates friction for recipients and increases the likelihood that important appendices or signatures get overlooked. Common merging scenarios include:

  • Job Applications: Combining your Cover Letter, Resume/CV, Professional Portfolio, and Letters of Recommendation into a single clean application package.
  • Tax Filings & Financial Accounting: Binding quarterly W-2s, 1099 statements, bank balances, and expense receipts into a single audit packet for your CPA.
  • Legal Bundles & Contracts: Appending exhibits, riders, and addendums directly behind the main signed contract agreement.
  • Academic Research Papers: Concatenating title pages, methodology chapters, bibliography references, and high-resolution chart appendices.

3. Handling Mixed Page Sizes & Orientations (Portrait + Landscape)

A common pitfall with basic PDF combining tools is that they force every page into a uniform portrait orientation, distorting wide landscape spreadsheets or technical architectural drawings.

Every page in a compliant PDF document contains its own explicit geometry dictionary:

  • /MediaBox: Defines the physical boundaries of the medium on which the page is intended to be displayed or printed (e.g. standard A4 at 595 x 842 points or US Letter at 612 x 792 points).
  • /Rotate: Dictates the visual rotation angle in degrees (0, 90, 180, or 270) clockwise.

The EcrioX merge engine preserves each page's specific /MediaBox and /Rotate dictionary, allowing portrait cover letters and 11x17 landscape financial projection sheets to coexist seamlessly in one file.
