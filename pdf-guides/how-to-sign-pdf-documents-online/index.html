1. Are Electronic Signatures Legal? (ESIGN, eIDAS, IT Act)

Yes. Modern global jurisprudence treats electronic signatures with the same legal validity and enforceability as handwritten wet-ink signatures:

  • United States: The Electronic Signatures in Global and National Commerce Act (ESIGN Act, 15 U.S.C. § 7001) and the Uniform Electronic Transactions Act (UETA) establish that a signature, contract, or other record relating to a transaction may not be denied legal effect solely because it is in electronic form.
  • European Union: The Electronic Identification, Authentication and Trust Services (eIDAS Regulation No 910/2014) mandates that an electronic signature shall not be denied legal effect and admissibility as evidence in legal proceedings solely because it is electronic.
  • India: The Information Technology Act 2000 recognizes electronic authentication and valid agreements executed digitally.
Key Rule of Enforceability: What makes an electronic signature valid in court is evidence of intent to sign, consent to do business electronically, and association of the mark with the document.

2. Electronic Signatures vs Cryptographic Digital Signatures

While the terms are often used interchangeably in marketing, they have distinct technical meanings:

Feature Standard Electronic Signature (e-Sign) Cryptographic Digital Signature (PKI)
Visual Appearance A drawn calligraphy mark, typed cursive name, or stamped image. Often accompanied by a digital certificate stamp or invisible cryptographic signature block.
Implementation Embedded vector or raster image stamped into the PDF content stream. Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) certificate issued by an accredited Certificate Authority (CA).
Best For Day-to-day business contracts, NDAs, rental leases, freelance scopes of work, and employment letters. Government defense contracts, official deeds, judicial filings requiring strict statutory PKI certificates.
