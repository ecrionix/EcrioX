🎯 Learning Objectives

By conducting this interactive experiment, students and educators will achieve the following pedagogical goals:

  • Demonstrate Kepler's 1st Law: Bound celestial orbits are ellipses with the central body at one focus.
  • Demonstrate Kepler's 2nd Law: A line segment joining planet and star sweeps out equal areas during equal intervals of time (orbital speed increases at periapsis).
  • Verify Kepler's 3rd Law: The square of orbital period is proportional to the cube of semi-major axis (T² ∝ a³).
  • Differentiate between circular velocity, elliptical bound states, and escape velocity.

⚒ Mathematical Equations & Theoretical Formulas

The simulation models the following governing physical relationships:

F_g = G\frac{M m}{r^2}, \quad v_{\text{circ}} = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}, \quad v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r}}, \quad T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{GM}a^3

Gravitational pull provides the exact centripetal force required for stable orbits. Specific orbital energy determines whether an orbit is bound (ellipse) or unbound (hyperbola).

📝 Step-by-Step Laboratory Procedure

  1. Step 1: Select the 'Circular Orbit' preset and note initial orbital radius r and calculated speed v = √(GM/r).
  2. Step 2: Click 'Start Orbit' and observe the circular trajectory with constant speed and radial gravitational force vectors.
  3. Step 3: Increase launch speed by 25% to transition into an elliptical orbit. Observe how the satellite speeds up at periapsis (closest point) and slows down at apoapsis (furthest point).
  4. Step 4: Increase velocity beyond escape velocity (v ≥ √2 · v_circ) to witness a hyperbolic escape trajectory leaving the gravity well permanently.

📖 Scientific Explanation & Physics Theory

In 1687, Isaac Newton unified terrestrial and celestial mechanics by proving that the same gravitational force causing an apple to fall also tethers the Moon to Earth. For a satellite in circular orbit, gravitational force F = GMm/r² equals centripetal force F = mv²/r, which yields circular orbital velocity v = √(GM/r). If kinetic energy is increased such that total mechanical energy E = ½mv² - GMm/r ≥ 0, the satellite enters an open parabolic or hyperbolic trajectory and permanently escapes the gravitational field of the central body at escape velocity v_esc = √(2GM/r).

🌎 Real-World Engineering Applications

  • Satellite communication: Placing geostationary satellites at exactly 35,786 km altitude to achieve a 24-hour orbital period matching Earth's rotation.
  • Interplanetary mission design: Computing Hohmann transfer ellipses to send spacecraft to Mars and Jupiter with minimum propellant consumption.
  • Exoplanet discovery: Detecting wobbling stars and planetary orbital periods using Kepler's Third Law.
  • Global Positioning System (GPS): Constellations of 31 satellites orbiting at 20,200 km providing global positioning signals.

💡 Worked Numerical Example

Practice Problem:

Calculate the orbital speed and orbital period of the International Space Station (ISS) orbiting at an altitude of 400 km above Earth's surface (Earth mass M = 5.972 × 10²⁴ kg, Earth radius R = 6,371 km, G = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²).

Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
1. Total orbital radius: r = R + h = 6,371,000 + 400,000 = 6,771,000 m = 6.771 × 10⁶ m.
2. Circular orbital speed: v = √(GM / r) = √[(6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ · 5.972 × 10²⁴) / (6.771 × 10⁶)] = √[(3.9857 × 10¹⁴) / (6.771 × 10⁶)] = √(58,864,274) ≈ 7,672 m/s (approx 27,600 km/h).
3. Orbital period: T = 2πr / v = (2 · π · 6.771 × 10⁶) / 7672 ≈ 5,546 seconds ≈ 92.4 minutes.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Astronauts are not outside Earth's gravity; at 400 km altitude, gravity is still about 90% of surface gravity. They experience weightlessness because both the space station and the astronauts are in continuous free fall toward Earth, falling at the same rate while moving horizontally fast enough to continuously curve around the planet.
Atmospheric drag reduces total mechanical energy. This causes the satellite's orbital radius to decrease. Paradoxically, as it spirals into a lower orbit with stronger gravitational potential, its orbital speed actually increases, while frictional heating eventually causes burning upon re-entry.
Escape velocity is exactly √2 (approximately 1.414) times the circular orbital speed at that same radius: v_esc = √2 · v_circ. An orbital speed boost of just 41.4% provides enough kinetic energy to completely overcome the central body's gravitational potential.
