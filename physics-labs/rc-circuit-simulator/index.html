🎯 Learning Objectives

By conducting this interactive experiment, students and educators will achieve the following pedagogical goals:

  • Calculate and experimentally verify the RC time constant τ = R·C in seconds.
  • Demonstrate that capacitor voltage approaches source voltage asymptotically during charging (Vc = V0(1 - e^(-t/RC))).
  • Show that charging current is maximum at t = 0 (I0 = V0/R) and decays exponentially to zero as the capacitor reaches full charge.
  • Analyze exponential discharge through a load resistor and calculate stored electrostatic energy U = ½CV².

⚒ Mathematical Equations & Theoretical Formulas

The simulation models the following governing physical relationships:

\tau = R \cdot C, \quad V_c(t) = V_0(1 - e^{-t/\tau}) \; [\text{Charge}], \quad V_c(t) = V_{\text{init}}e^{-t/\tau} \; [\text{Discharge}]

At t = τ (one time constant), capacitor charges to 63.2% of source voltage during charging, or discharges to 36.8% of initial voltage during discharging. Stored energy U = ½CV².

📝 Step-by-Step Laboratory Procedure

  1. Step 1: Set DC voltage V0 to 10 V, resistor R to 10 kΩ (10,000 Ω), and capacitor C to 100 µF (0.0001 F).
  2. Step 2: Calculate expected time constant: τ = R · C = 10,000 · 0.0001 = 1.00 second.
  3. Step 3: Flip the switch to 'Charge' and start the timer. Observe charge particles collecting on the plates and voltage rising.
  4. Step 4: Inspect the live graph at t = 1.0 s to verify that capacitor voltage reaches exactly 6.32 V (63.2% of 10 V).
  5. Step 5: At t = 5.0 s (5τ), observe that the capacitor is virtually fully charged (99.3%) and current has dropped to near zero.
  6. Step 6: Flip the switch to 'Discharge' and observe voltage decaying back toward zero.

📖 Scientific Explanation & Physics Theory

A capacitor stores electric potential energy in the electrostatic field established between two separated conductors. In an RC series circuit connected to a DC source V0, Kirchhoff's Voltage Law dictates: V0 = I(t)R + Vc(t) = R(dq/dt) + q/C. Solving this first-order linear differential equation yields the exponential charging relation Vc(t) = V0(1 - e^(-t/RC)). Initially, when the capacitor is uncharged (Vc = 0), it acts like an ideal short circuit, and initial current is bounded solely by Ohm's Law (I0 = V0/R). As charge Q = CV accumulates, the opposing capacitor voltage reduces current flow until the capacitor behaves like an open circuit (I = 0) at steady state.

🌎 Real-World Engineering Applications

  • Power supply smoothing filters: Smoothing rectified AC ripple into stable DC voltages in smartphone chargers and computers.
  • Timing and pulse generator circuits: 555 timer ICs and clock oscillators relying on RC charge/discharge thresholds.
  • Medical defibrillators: Storing hundreds of joules of electrical energy slowly in high-voltage capacitors and discharging it rapidly across a patient's heart.
  • Camera flashes: Charging an internal capacitor from low-voltage batteries to power high-intensity xenon flash tubes.

💡 Worked Numerical Example

Practice Problem:

An uncharged 470 µF capacitor is connected in series with a 47 kΩ resistor to a 12 V DC battery. Calculate: (a) Time constant τ, (b) Initial charging current at t = 0, (c) Capacitor voltage at t = 22 seconds, (d) Total energy stored when fully charged.

Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
1. Time constant: τ = R · C = (47 × 10³ Ω) · (470 × 10⁻⁶ F) = 22.09 seconds.
2. Initial current: I0 = V0 / R = 12 / (47,000) ≈ 0.255 mA = 2.55 × 10⁻⁴ A.
3. Voltage at t = 22.09 s (t = 1τ): Vc(τ) = 12 · (1 - e⁻¹) = 12 · (1 - 0.3679) = 12 · 0.6321 ≈ 7.585 Volts.
4. Stored energy when fully charged: U = ½ · C · V² = 0.5 · (470 × 10⁻⁶) · (12)² = 0.5 · 470 × 10⁻⁶ · 144 ≈ 0.03384 Joules (33.84 mJ).

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

The time constant τ = RC represents the time required for a capacitor to charge to approximately 63.2% of the applied voltage, or discharge to 36.8% of its initial voltage. By convention, 5 time constants (5τ) is considered the time needed to reach practical steady state (99.3% charged).
Initially, the uncharged capacitor has zero potential difference across its plates, so the full source voltage drops across the resistor (I = V0/R). As electrons accumulate, the capacitor develops an opposing voltage Vc. The effective voltage across the resistor is V0 - Vc, which gradually decreases, driving current down to zero.
In an ideal theoretical circuit with R = 0, time constant τ = 0 and charging would be instantaneous with infinite initial current. In real circuits, connecting wires and battery internal resistance prevent instantaneous charging and limit peak current.
