🎯 Learning Objectives

By conducting this interactive experiment, students and educators will achieve the following pedagogical goals:

  • Demonstrate independence of horizontal motion (constant velocity) and vertical motion (constant acceleration).
  • Verify that maximum horizontal range occurs at an angle of 45° for level ground launches.
  • Analyze how varying gravitational acceleration (Earth vs Moon vs Mars) scales trajectory geometry.
  • Compare numerical simulated flight times and apex heights with theoretical kinematic formulas.

⚒ Mathematical Equations & Theoretical Formulas

The simulation models the following governing physical relationships:

R = \frac{u^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g}, \quad H = \frac{u^2 \sin^2(\theta)}{2g}, \quad T = \frac{2u \sin(\theta)}{g}

Horizontal position x(t) = u cos(θ)·t; Vertical position y(t) = u sin(θ)·t - ½gt²; Maximum range R occurs at 45° launch angle under uniform level ground conditions.

📝 Step-by-Step Laboratory Procedure

  1. Step 1: Select an initial velocity (e.g., 25 m/s) using the speed slider.
  2. Step 2: Set the launch angle to 45° and select Earth gravity (9.8 m/s²).
  3. Step 3: Click 'Launch Projectile' and observe the trajectory path and velocity vectors.
  4. Step 4: Record the maximum height (H), total horizontal range (R), and flight time (T) from the measurement HUD.
  5. Step 5: Repeat the experiment at complementary launch angles (e.g., 30° and 60°) to verify that complementary angles yield identical horizontal ranges.

📖 Scientific Explanation & Physics Theory

Projectile motion is a form of two-dimensional motion where an object is thrown into a gravitational field and moves along a curved path under the action of gravity alone. Galileo Galilei first demonstrated that projectile trajectories are parabolic. Horizontal velocity remains constant (vx = u cos θ) because no horizontal forces act on the projectile (neglecting air resistance). Vertical velocity decreases linearly under gravity (vy = u sin θ - gt), reaches zero at the trajectory apex, and accelerates downward until impact. The maximum range formula R = (u² sin 2θ)/g reveals that sin(2θ) reaches its theoretical maximum of 1 when 2θ = 90°, or θ = 45°.

💡 Worked Numerical Example

Practice Problem:

A soccer ball is kicked with an initial velocity of 20 m/s at an angle of 30° above the horizontal on Earth (g = 9.8 m/s²). Calculate: (a) Time of flight, (b) Maximum height, (c) Horizontal range.

Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
1. Initial velocity components: ux = 20 · cos(30°) = 20 · 0.8660 = 17.32 m/s; uy = 20 · sin(30°) = 20 · 0.5000 = 10.00 m/s.
2. Time of flight: T = 2uy / g = (2 · 10) / 9.8 = 20 / 9.8 ≈ 2.04 s.
3. Maximum height: H = uy² / (2g) = (10)² / (2 · 9.8) = 100 / 19.6 ≈ 5.10 m.
4. Range: R = ux · T = 17.32 · 2.04 ≈ 35.35 m (or R = 20² · sin(60°) / 9.8 = 400 · 0.866 / 9.8 ≈ 35.35 m).

Range and maximum height are inversely proportional to gravity (R ∝ 1/g, H ∝ 1/g). Because the Moon's gravity (1.62 m/s²) is approximately one-sixth of Earth's, a ball launched with the same velocity on the Moon will travel approximately six times further and six times higher.
