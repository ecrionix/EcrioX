🎯 Learning Objectives

By conducting this interactive experiment, students and educators will achieve the following pedagogical goals:

  • Construct principal ray diagrams for convex lenses, concave lenses, concave mirrors, and convex mirrors.
  • Apply the thin lens formula 1/f = 1/v - 1/u and mirror formula 1/f = 1/v + 1/u with strict sign conventions.
  • Determine image characteristics (real vs virtual, inverted vs upright, magnified vs diminished).
  • Demonstrate how positioning an object at the focal point produces rays parallel to the axis (image at infinity).

⚒ Mathematical Equations & Theoretical Formulas

The simulation models the following governing physical relationships:

\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} \; [\text{Mirror}], \quad \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} \; [\text{Lens}], \quad m = -\frac{v}{u} \; [\text{Mirror}], \quad m = +\frac{v}{u} \; [\text{Lens}]

Cartesian sign convention: Incident light travels from left to right. Distances measured against incident light are negative; distances measured along incident light are positive.

📝 Step-by-Step Laboratory Procedure

  1. Step 1: Select 'Convex Lens' mode and set focal length f = 15 cm.
  2. Step 2: Place the object at u = -30 cm (at 2F). Observe that the real inverted image forms at exactly v = +30 cm (at 2F') with equal size (|m| = 1.0).
  3. Step 3: Move the object closer between 2F and F (e.g., u = -20 cm). Observe that the image moves beyond 2F' and becomes magnified (|m| > 1).
  4. Step 4: Move the object inside the focal point (u = -10 cm). Observe the transition from real rays to dashed virtual extension rays forming a magnified, upright virtual image on the same side as the object (magnifying glass effect).
  5. Step 5: Switch to 'Concave Mirror' and repeat the observations.

📖 Scientific Explanation & Physics Theory

Geometrical optics treats light propagation as rectilinear rays obeying the laws of reflection (angle of incidence equals angle of reflection) and refraction (Snell's Law: n1 sin θ1 = n2 sin θ2). Three principal rays determine the position and size of an image formed by thin lenses: (1) A ray parallel to the principal axis refracts through the focal point; (2) A ray passing through the optical center continues undeflected; (3) A ray passing through the front focus emerges parallel to the axis. When refracted rays physically converge, they form a real image that can be projected onto a screen. When rays diverge, extending them backward locates a virtual image.

🌎 Real-World Engineering Applications

  • Eyeglasses and contact lenses: Correcting myopia (nearsightedness) with diverging concave lenses and hyperopia (farsightedness) with converging convex lenses.
  • Optical microscopes and astronomical telescopes: Combining objective and eyepiece lenses to achieve high angular magnification of microscopic specimens and distant celestial bodies.
  • Smartphone camera lens assemblies: Multi-element precision optical arrays focusing scenes onto CMOS electronic image sensors.
  • Solar concentrators and satellite dishes: Parabolic concave reflectors focusing parallel incoming electromagnetic waves to a focal collector point.

💡 Worked Numerical Example

Practice Problem:

An object 4.0 cm tall is placed 25.0 cm in front of a converging (convex) lens with a focal length of 15.0 cm. Using the Cartesian sign convention (u = -25 cm, f = +15 cm), calculate: (a) Image position v, (b) Linear magnification m, (c) Image height hi, (d) Nature of the image.

Step-by-Step Analytical Solution:
1. Lens formula: 1/f = 1/v - 1/u ⇒ 1/v = 1/f + 1/u = 1/(15) + 1/(-25) = (5 - 3) / 75 = 2 / 75.
2. Image distance: v = 75 / 2 = +37.5 cm (positive sign indicates image is real, formed on the opposite side of the lens).
3. Magnification: m = v / u = (+37.5) / (-25.0) = -1.50.
4. Image height: hi = m · ho = -1.50 · 4.0 = -6.0 cm.
5. Image nature: REAL (v > 0), INVERTED (m < 0), and MAGNIFIED (|m| = 1.5 > 1).

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

A real image is formed by the actual physical intersection of light rays and can be projected onto a physical screen. A virtual image is formed where light rays only appear to diverge from when extended backward; it cannot be projected onto a screen but can be seen directly by the human eye.
When an object is placed closer to a convex lens than its focal point (object distance u < focal length f), the refracted rays diverge. Tracing them backward reveals an upright, magnified virtual image located behind the object, allowing the human eye to view an enlarged image at comfortable focusing distances.
When u = -f, the lens formula yields 1/v = 1/f - 1/f = 0, which means v = ∞. The refracted rays emerge completely parallel to one another and never converge at a finite distance, forming an image at infinity (used in searchlights and collimators).
