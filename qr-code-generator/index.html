What is a QR Code?

A QR Code (Quick Response Code) is a two-dimensional matrix barcode originally developed in 1994 by the Japanese engineering firm Denso Wave. Unlike traditional one-dimensional barcodes that encode information only along a single horizontal axis, QR codes store data both vertically and horizontally in a matrix of square modules.

This two-dimensional architecture allows QR codes to store up to 7,089 numeric characters or 4,296 alphanumeric characters, compared to roughly 20 digits in a standard retail barcode.

Static vs. Dynamic QR Codes

It is critical for business owners and developers to understand the distinction between static and dynamic QR codes:

  • Static QR Codes (Generated by EcrioX): The destination URL or data payload is encoded directly into the pixel pattern itself. Static QR codes never expire, require zero subscriptions, have no scan limits, and function permanently without contacting any intermediary server. Because processing occurs entirely inside your browser, sensitive Wi-Fi passwords and personal contact details are never transmitted over the network.
  • Dynamic QR Codes: The QR code encodes a short redirect link hosted on an external server, allowing the final destination to be modified later and tracking scan analytics. However, if the hosting service ceases operation or imposes a paywall, dynamic QR codes stop working immediately.
