2.4 GHz vs 5 GHz Channel Spectrum Overlap

Active Channel: CH 36

On 2.4 GHz, channels are 20 MHz wide with only 5 MHz spacing. Only channels 1, 6, and 11 do not overlap. On 5 GHz, channels operate with wider 40/80/160 MHz clean separation.

Practical Optimization Recommendations

  • Switch to 5 GHz for bandwidth-intensive tasks: Your selected 5 GHz band avoids microwave and Bluetooth interference prevalent on 2.4 GHz.
  • Elevate your router: Place the Wi-Fi router on a desk or shelf 1.5 meters off the ground rather than behind a television or near metal pipes.
  • Avoid non-standard 2.4 GHz channels: If using 2.4 GHz, stick strictly to channels 1, 6, or 11 to avoid adjacent channel interference from neighboring routers.

Why Can't Browsers Scan All Nearby Wi-Fi Networks?

For user security and location privacy, standard W3C browser specifications strictly prohibit web pages from querying low-level 802.11 network hardware or sniffing nearby router BSSID MAC addresses. Web applications claiming to "scan all neighborhood Wi-Fi routers" are fabricating mock data. EcrioX provides genuine browser telemetry alongside our verified manual RF diagnostic engine above.

Wi-Fi Signal Strength (dBm) Classification Guide

In radio frequency physics, signal is expressed as decibels relative to one milliwatt (dBm). Because numbers are negative, values closer to zero indicate stronger signal power.

Signal (dBm) Classification Real-World Performance Recommended Use Cases
-30 to -50 dBm Excellent Maximum theoretical link speed, zero packet loss 4K/8K video streaming, high-speed file transfers, competitive gaming
-51 to -60 dBm Very Good Strong, stable connection with high throughput HD video conferencing, simultaneous multi-device streaming
-61 to -67 dBm Good Minimum recommended standard for reliable VoIP & Zoom Web browsing, video calls, music streaming, office work

Understanding Wi-Fi Signals & Frequency Bands

Wireless networks transmit data using high-frequency electromagnetic radio waves. Understanding frequency bands and channel management is essential for optimizing wireless performance:

  • 2.4 GHz Band: Longer wavelengths penetrate through solid brick walls and floors efficiently. However, the spectrum only accommodates three non-overlapping 20 MHz channels (Channels 1, 6, and 11) and suffers heavy interference from baby monitors, Bluetooth, and microwaves.
  • 5 GHz Band: Provides significantly wider channels (40 MHz, 80 MHz, and 160 MHz) and much higher throughput with lower latency. However, 5 GHz signals attenuate rapidly through solid concrete and drywall.
  • 6 GHz Band (Wi-Fi 6E & Wi-Fi 7): Opens up to 1,200 MHz of pristine spectrum with up to seven ultra-wide 160 MHz channels or three 320 MHz channels. Operates with zero legacy Wi-Fi 4/5 interference, ideal for VR and multi-gigabit connections.
